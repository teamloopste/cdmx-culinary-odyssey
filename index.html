✈ Day 5 — Departure

Slow Morning • Transfer

A leisurely breakfast at the hotel or a nearby café. One last stroll through Roma’s tree-lined streets. Private transfer to the airport. You leave carrying the flavors, the light, and the ease of a trip that felt entirely yours.

Peanut Allergy Protocol (Built In): Every guide and restaurant is briefed in advance. We provide a Spanish allergy card (“Tengo una alergia grave a los cacahuates…”). Focus on tacos, grilled meats, ceviche-style, simple preparations, and places that confirm no cross-contact. Avoid all moles, pipianes, and salsa macha. Carry epinephrine. Fine dining and taquerías where assembly is visible are prioritized.

Culinary Philosophy

Mexico City is one of the world’s great food cities. We go deep, safely, and with local expertise.

  • • Private or chef-hosted food tours over generic group tours
  • • Markets with guidance (Medellín preferred over larger ones for safety and control)
  • • Mezcal education — small-batch, respectful tasting
  • • Mix of street-level authenticity and elevated tables
  • • All experiences allergy-briefed in advance
Safe Defaults: Tacos al pastor, suadero, or asada with controlled toppings. Salsa verde over roja when unsure. Ceviche and grilled fish at places that confirm no nuts. Always show the card. Many excellent restaurants in Roma (modern Mexican, Italian-Mexican, seafood) handle allergies well when notified.

Practical Guide & Safety

Safety First

Tourist neighborhoods (Roma, Condesa, Polanco, Centro by day) are safe with standard big-city awareness. Main risk is petty theft. Never hail street taxis. Use Uber or DiDi exclusively. Stick together as a group, especially at night. Our itinerary avoids anything remotely shady.

Transport

Uber/DiDi for all door-to-door. Private transfers for airport and day trips. Walking within Roma/Condesa is a joy. Metro is fine by day for longer hops if desired, but we prioritize comfort and ease for a luxury-leaning trip.

Altitude & Practical

Ease into Day 1. Stay hydrated. Bottled water. Best time: March–May (jacarandas) or dry season. Cash for markets/tips; cards widely accepted. Spanish phrases help but English is common in tourist/food scenes.

Shopping & Sightseeing: Roma and Condesa for design, fashion, and local brands. Centro for classic souvenirs and architecture. Day trips add the “wow” without complexity. We keep pacing relaxed so the food and friendship remain the focus.

Deals & How to Book Smart

Luxury feel without the full luxury price tag. These strategies keep you near the $4,000 CAD group target.

🏨 Hotels

Book direct for 10–20% off, free breakfast, or credits. Watch for advance-purchase rates. Roma boutiques often beat Polanco five-stars on value.

🎭 Experiences

GetYourGuide, Viator, or direct with local operators for private group rates. Chef-led food tours frequently $80–150 pp — share the cost. Ask for private if group of 4+.

✈ Flights (from Canada)

Look for midweek or shoulder season. Direct from YYZ can be competitive. Flair Airlines for lowest-cost directs. Also check Air Canada, Aeromexico, Skyscanner, Google Flights. Flexibility helps.

✦ Extras

Many museums free on Sundays. Mercado lunches are inexpensive and excellent. Mezcal bars have happy hours. Tips are expected but modest.
